\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Discrete approaches to quantum gravity, from Regge calculus
\citep{regge1961} to causal dynamical triangulations
\citep{ambjorn2012cdt}, replace smooth spacetime with combinatorial data:
simplices, gluings, and the lengths assigned to them.
In Regge calculus the combinatorial scaffold is fixed with only
edge lengths allowed to vary. The vertices, simplices, and gluings of the triangulation
never change, so no new combinatorial structure is ever introduced. That is
enough for classical gravity, where one solves the field equations on a
triangulation that is given in advance. The discomfort begins one step
later, when Feynman's path integral is applied to the evolution of the
universe and the sum runs over all histories. Summing over Regge geometries
means integrating over edge lengths, a continuous space in which many
assignments describe the same geometry and no canonical measure presents
itself. On the other hand, dynamical triangulations
\citep{david1985,ambjorn1985diseases,ambjorn1997quantum} escape this by
inverting Regge's choice.
The lengths are frozen and the combinatorics is set free, so a geometry
becomes a gluing pattern of simplices whose shapes are fixed once and for
all, and summing over geometries becomes counting gluings.

In both cases the geometry is carried by the gluing and not by any ambient
space. Each simplex is flat inside, so curvature can only sit where simplices
meet, and it is measured by how far the angles around such a meeting place
fall short of a full turn. Figure~\ref{fig:deficit} shows the three cases for
triangles glued around a vertex.

Because the combinatorics is now free, the number of simplices differs from
history to history, and within a single history the number of simplices in a
slice changes from one slice to the next. New geometric measure appears from
nowhere. Classical general relativity is no better off on this point, since
it has no conservation law for spatial volume either, and in a closed
universe the Hamiltonian constraint balances the total at zero
\citep{adm1962,dewitt1967} however much volume appears. The complaint is
therefore not that volume grows, but that the growth answers to nothing the
theory conserves. A cosmological constant term only assigns a cost to
four-volume within the ensemble, and where the total is held fixed instead,
as in simulations and in unimodular gravity \citep{unruh1989,henneaux1989},
the constraint is imposed by hand rather than descending from a symmetry.

This paper takes the discomfort seriously and asks what a theory looks like if
one demands, from the start, that

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Nothing is ever destroyed. Evolution proceeds only by forking
        and completion moves, never by deletion, so that topological
        invariants are conserved by construction.
  \item Time is not a dimension of the fundamental structure but an emergent
        bookkeeping of an irreversible forking-filling process.
  \item Entropy is attached to frame-invariant causal data, not to spatial
        slices, so that the theory never needs to say what all of space
        looks like at a single moment.
  \item The rule that chooses which move happens next is derived from an
        inference principle rather than postulated.
\end{enumerate}

The framework we arrive at is simple to state. At any moment, the state of
the universe is a finite simplicial set with directed edges, together with
the history of how it was built. It evolves by two local moves. A
\textbf{sprout} attaches a fresh directed edge ending at a new vertex,
adding new space. A \textbf{fill} completes an unfilled inner horn,
attaching a composite edge together with the higher cell that witnesses the
composition. The limit of the filling process is a quasicategory, a
structure in which every composition has been formed while the edges remain
directed. The classical, zero-information limit is the nerve of an ordinary
category, in which every filler is unique. The physical content of a solved
universe is precisely how far it is from being a nerve.

Forgetting the directions and the history leaves a bare homotopy type.
Every move is a weak homotopy equivalence, so this type never changes; it
remains that of a point, and everything physical lives in the directed
structure that this forgetting erases. The theory also carries a mirror
symmetry. Reversing every edge sends each universe to its mirror image, and
the universe carries an arrow of time exactly when it is not isomorphic to
this image. The big bang vertex is its own mirror image, so the arrow is
not present at the beginning; it appears as the growing structure breaks
this symmetry. Pairing each universe with its mirror image gives a total
object that is symmetric as a whole, in the spirit of CPT-symmetric
cosmologies.

Each move is an event. An event depends on exactly those earlier events that
created the cells it consumed, and this dependency relation is a partial
order that defines causality. A reference frame is nothing but a linearization of
this order. The central quantitative object of the paper is the entropy
$$
  S(e) \;=\; \log \#\{\text{linear extensions of } \past{e}\},
$$
the logarithm of the number of ways the causal past of an event could have
been sequenced. This functional is foliation-invariant by construction,
monotone under the dynamics, and measures causal parallelism, which is a fully
sequential past has zero entropy; a fully parallel past of $n$ events has
entropy $\log n!$.

Our contributions are: (i) a precise state space and move set satisfying the
conservation demands above (Section~\ref{sec:framework}); (ii) the
causal-past entropy functional together with statements (and, where complete,
proofs) of its basic properties (Section~\ref{sec:entropy}); (iii) a
formulation of the maximum-entropy inference problem that is intended to
derive the dynamical rule (Section~\ref{sec:maxent}); (iv) an argument
that the combinatorial escalation of coherence questions forces decelerating
expansion (Section~\ref{sec:tower}); (v) first numerical results from a toy
implementation (Section~\ref{sec:results}); and (vi) an explicit map of the
design space, recording the alternatives rejected at each decision point so
that they remain available for future exploration
(Section~\ref{sec:designspace}).

We emphasise the register of this paper: it is a framework proposal
with a small number of proved statements, one computed experiment, and a
list of hard open problems (Section~\ref{sec:open}), of which the recovery
of the full Lorentz group from causal invariance, and of an area law for the
entropy of causal horizons, are the most severe. Where a proof is incomplete
the text says so, visibly.
