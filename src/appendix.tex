\section{The budget triangle}
\label{app:budget}

Let the move rate of any subsystem be $c$ (Axiom~\ref{ax:budget}) and suppose
a subsystem translates at speed $v$. Writing the split as orthogonal
components, the internal rate $r$ satisfies $r^2 + v^2 = c^2$, hence
\[
  r \;=\; c\,\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2},
\]
the standard dilation factor, with the photon limit $r = 0$ at $v = c$.

The orthogonal composition is an assumption and not a consequence of the
move set. Counting moves gives a linear split instead. A track of $N$ events
of which $k$ are internal has $v/c = (N-k)/N$ and internal rate $k/N$, so
that $r = c\,(1 - v/c)$, which differs from the expression above by a factor
of three at $v = 0.8c$. Recovering the quadratic form requires the emergent
geometry of linearizations to be Lorentzian rather than Euclidean, which is
to say that straight worldlines must maximise accumulated internal filling.
That is the content of problem O1, and Definition~\ref{def:propertime} is
framed so that the required statement is the known longest-chain theorem for
sprinklings rather than a new assumption.
