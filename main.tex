\large A Framework for Emergent Time from Horn-Filling Dynamics}

\begin{abstract}
We propose a framework in which spacetime is not fundamental. The primitive
ontology is a growing simplicial set with directed edges, evolving by two local,
non-deleting rewriting moves: \emph{sprouting} (creation of a fresh directed
edge to a new vertex) and \emph{inner-horn filling} (attachment of a composite
and its witnessing cell). Moves are events; their dependency structure defines a
causal partial order, and a reference frame is a linearization of this order.
Time is not a dimension but the process of horn filling; the fully-solved limit
object is a quasicategory. We define a foliation-invariant entropy
$S(e) = \log \#\{\text{linear extensions of } \past{e}\}$ attached to causal
pasts rather than spatial slices, prove its monotonicity, and present first
numerical results from a toy implementation showing that entropy growth is
controlled by the sprout-to-fill ratio. The dynamical rule itself is not
postulated: we formulate a maximum-entropy inference problem whose solution,
conjecturally of Gibbs form, determines the rule from locality and a fixed
update budget alone. We further argue that the escalation of coherence
questions up the associahedral tower forces decelerating, power-law-like
expansion. We map the design space of alternatives at every decision point and
state the open problems, of which the recovery of the Lorentz group and of an
area law for horizon entropy are the most severe.
\end{abstract}
